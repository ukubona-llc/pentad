Governance failure at V corrupts everything downstream.
Bias and weights are the compressed eigenmode of the training data — they are safe to publish. The rows that produced them are gone. So why does the moat hold? Three reasons, in order of honesty.
01 · REAL
The training data was expensive
The weights are cheap to copy. The corpus that shaped them — licensed, scraped, curated at billions in compute and legal cost — is the actual asset. Publishing weights gives away the eigenmode without surrendering the eigenspace. Meta knew this: Llama ships without the training data.
02 · REAL
Alignment is a thin layer — fine-tuning strips it
The 65 GB Llama download is the attack surface. Base capability travels in the weights; RLHF alignment is a shallow residual on top. Fine-tune for a few hours and the refusals are gone. Publishing weights = publishing capability with no guarantee the safety layer travels.
03 · MARGINAL
Membership inference from weights
You cannot reconstruct a training record from weights — but you can sometimes confirm a specific record was present. For models trained on medical or legal data, that confirmation is itself a privacy violation. The weights carry a shadow of the data, not the data itself.
The real governance surface is fine-tuning, not weights. Closed weights is a leaky abstraction — the eigenmode leaks through API outputs anyway, and distillation lets anyone approximate it cheaply. The question that matters: who controls the update? Who gets to fine-tune, on what data, toward what objective? That is the S-layer problem. The moat was never at V.
